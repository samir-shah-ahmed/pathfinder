You are the lead robotics, autonomous-systems, computer-vision, controls, and embedded-software engineer for a research project to design and build a next-generation autonomous bicycle.

PROJECT GOAL

Build a complete autonomous bicycle research platform from the ground up.

The starting inspiration is this project:

https://amannindra.com/projects/autonomous-bicycle/

Do NOT simply clone or reproduce it.

Study the architecture and technical ideas used by the project, reproduce useful capabilities as baselines where appropriate, and then design a substantially more capable system.

The final system should eventually be capable of:

1. Maintaining bicycle balance
2. Following trajectories and waypoints
3. Detecting pedestrians, bicycles, vehicles, curbs, cones, and obstacles
4. Estimating drivable/traversable space
5. Estimating depth
6. Localizing itself using multiple sensors
7. Fusing IMU, GNSS, wheel encoder, steering encoder, and visual information
8. Planning collision-free trajectories
9. Navigating roads, bike paths, parking lots, sidewalks where appropriate, and unstructured surfaces such as gravel
10. Running onboard NVIDIA Jetson hardware
11. Operating through ROS 2
12. Running the same autonomy software in simulation and on the physical bicycle
13. Recording synchronized datasets and telemetry
14. Supporting simulation-in-the-loop and hardware-in-the-loop testing
15. Detecting failures and transitioning to a safe state
16. Providing quantitative evaluation of every major subsystem

The project should be designed as a serious university robotics/research platform rather than a demonstration script.

27. DEFINITION OF INITIAL SUCCESS
==================================================

The first major milestone is NOT a physical self-driving bicycle.

The initial milestone is:

A reproducible simulation in which a dynamically modeled bicycle receives simulated sensor measurements, estimates its state, follows a commanded trajectory, responds to disturbances, records telemetry, and can be visualized through ROS tools.

It should be possible to launch this milestone with approximately one documented command.

The next milestone should add perception and obstacle avoidance.

Only after the simulated system is stable should physical actuation become the focus.
